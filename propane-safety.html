If you suspect a propane gas leak

A suspected gas leak is an emergency. Propane can ignite and cause fire or explosion.

1. Leave immediately

Get everyone out of the building or area where a leak may be present.

2. Avoid sparks

Do not operate light switches, appliances, electrical devices, or phones inside the affected area. Do not smoke or use flames.

3. Report it from safety

Once safely away, call your propane supplier, 911, or the fire department. Do not return until cleared by qualified personnel.

Only if safe to do so and the leak is not suspected at the tank, turn off the tank service valve clockwise. Before restarting appliances, have the propane system checked by your supplier or a qualified professional.

Recognizing a possible propane leak

Warning signs can include the distinctive odor added to propane, a propane gas detector alarm, hissing near equipment, or unexplained gas use. Never ignore a suspected leak because you cannot smell gas.

Why you might not smell propane

Odor may settle in low areas or be carried away by airflow. Illness, sleep, age, competing odors, or prolonged exposure can reduce odor detection. Odor can also fade when propane contacts certain materials.

Propane gas detectors

Use detectors designed to sense propane and follow their manufacturer's placement and maintenance instructions. A propane detector is not the same as a smoke or carbon monoxide detector. Treat an alarm as an emergency even without a noticeable odor.

Safe propane appliance use

  • Follow appliance manufacturers' operating and maintenance instructions.
  • Keep combustion-air openings, exhaust vents, and surrounding areas clear.
  • Keep gasoline, solvents, combustible materials, and flammable vapors away from propane appliances.
  • Do not kink, strain, or damage flexible appliance connectors when moving or cleaning equipment.
  • Have a qualified professional cap or plug a gas line whenever an appliance is disconnected.
  • If a pilot light repeatedly goes out or is difficult to light, stop and contact a qualified professional; never attempt to troubleshoot a suspected leak.

When to call a qualified professional

Do not install, relocate, modify, or repair propane tanks, piping, regulators, appliance connectors, or gas appliances yourself. Arrange professional help for new installations, disconnections, repairs, scheduled maintenance, and signs of improper operation such as soot or blocked vents.

After a gas outage or shutoff: a qualified professional must check the system for leaks before service is restored. Do not simply turn the gas back on yourself.

Carbon monoxide (CO) safety

Carbon monoxide is a colorless, odorless poisonous gas that can cause serious injury or death. Faulty appliances, blocked vents, and inadequate combustion air can create dangerous conditions.

If you suspect carbon monoxide: leave the building immediately, call 911 or the fire department from a safe location, and do not return until emergency responders or qualified personnel say it is safe.

  • Install and maintain listed carbon monoxide alarms according to manufacturer instructions.
  • Have gas appliances inspected and maintained by qualified professionals.
  • Keep chimneys, vents, air intakes, and exhaust openings unobstructed.
  • Never use a gas oven, range, or outdoor grill to heat indoor spaces.
  • Watch for soot, unusual appliance odors, or excessive condensation that may signal improper operation.
